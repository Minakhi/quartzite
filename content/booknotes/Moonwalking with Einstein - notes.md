## Insights
  
The key to remembering something for longer:
1. take a moment to be really mindful of it
2. attach an emotion or a strong image to it
3. make a mental note to remember it  
  
**Visual Memory** is evolutionarily strong because it was a key survival skill. The more bizarre and non-recurring an image, the more memorable it is.  
  
**Spatial Memory**, similarly, is evolutionarily strong. Locus points within a space are extremely memorable. Hence, keeping vivid, bizarre images at loci within a known space is a highly potent memory technique.  
  
**Narrative Memory** and **Music Memory**, though relatively new in the evolutionary sense, are still powerful. Stories set to a tune (epic poems, folk songs, etc.) were the earliest form of mass transmission of ideas, insights, and lessons.  
  
**Memory works best for things you are really interested in**. For a chess grandmaster, chess positions are far more memorable than phone numbers. For a historian, dates are far more memorable than chess positions.  
  
**Memory skills are only loosely transferable**. A strong memory in a subject (chess positions) does not translate to superior memory in other subjects (finance, historical dates, languages, etc.)  
  
**Memory and Creativity feed each other**. The Latin root inventio lends itself to both inventory and invention. To invent something new, first you need an inventory of what is already known. The better your inventory, the better your chances of inventing beyond it. Also, a wide and deep inventory of ideas opens up the possibility of cross-breeding, which boosts the likelihood of a new thought emerging at the weighted intersection of the inventoried ideas.  
  
## Training Memory

**Memory, by itself, is not really a trainable muscle. But the art of visualization is.** The more you mindfully practice visualization, the easier it becomes, and the shorter it takes to make a moment/object/person/fact memorable. Similarly, developing a practice of encoding the same thing into memory in two or three different ways can help build more neural connections, and so improve your chances of retrieving the memory.  
  
**You can break through the OK Plateau**, the point beyond which our mind thinks it is OK not to improve in a skill, by deliberate practice. Measure indicators, keep a scorecard mapping lead KPIs with lag KPIs, isolate factors that you are weak in, and build on them. The shorter the feedback loop the better  
  
People learn any skill in 3 steps: 
1. **Cognitive**: when they get an intellectual understanding of the skill, acquire an awareness of the knobs and levers involved, and design a repeatable action timetable for practising. This stage determines the "level" of proficiency in the skill.  
2. **Associative**: when they start practising each of the learning actions regularly, making it part of their daily awareness. The mind then starts associating those actions with that skill. One gets faster at doing the same things over and over and pushes past the motivation dip that comes once the novelty of the cognitive step wears off. This is also where you learn to do things efficiently at that level, minimizing errors.  
3. **Autonomous**: when you can do the task without even consciously paying attention to it. Like riding a bicycle or brushing your teeth. The downside is that you are trapped at a certain level of proficiency when a skill becomes autonomous.  

What separates experts from the average is their ability and systems that keep returning them to the cognitive stage when they feel they have gotten something down in the autonomous stage.

## Deliberate Practice

1. **Train visualization with a method**: Pick any one method above and practice daily. Use a metronome to progressively improve beyond the OK plateau.  
2. **Train visualization on abstract words**: Make a list of emotions and terms, build images that would remind you of them, and keep going through that list to make the associations stronger.  
3. **Train mind-mapping**: After reading an article or a book that you want to remember, build a memory map of that from scratch. The act of building the map is the mindful step that will embed the memory deeper. Also, each mind-map will take mere minutes to traverse, hence serving as an effective refresher.
