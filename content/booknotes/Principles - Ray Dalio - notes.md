## Tools at Bridgewater Associates
  
#### Issue Log:  
Maintain a detailed log of all the problems faced. Problems and issues recur frequently. When faced with a problem, it helps to know that it is "just another one of those" that we have faced in the past. So, listing how we dealt with the problem, what worked and what didn't, where our blindspots were, where our biases lay, will be a good starting point to evaluate the problem at hand.  
  
**My version of this log**:  
- What was the problem (expectation vs reality)?  
- Who were involved with the problem?  
- What actions were taken to dissect the problem?  
- Which of these actions worked and why? Which didn't and why?  
- Did we suffer from any bias that we later discovered?  
- Did we have blindspots that we recognized?  
- What were the underlying principles and value systems we relied on to take the final decision?  
- Who was accountable for the final decision?  
- What were the different actions and their goals? Did we achieve them?  
- Could we have done this better with the additional perspective we have from hindsight? Can that be templatized for future problems?  
- What are some tell-tale signs that we can look for regularly to prevent such a problem from recurring, or to give us fair warning should they recur?  
  
  
#### Principles Log:  
For every decision taken, knowing the situation, the people involved, and the value system that led to the final decision is critical information that can help find patterns, and inform future decisions.  
  
  
#### Checklists:  
For predictable, repeatable tasks, having checklists helps break work down, assign to people, measure project progress, find process flaws, and diagnose problems by isolating steps where they occur.
