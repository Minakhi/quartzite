- The Stoics regarded speculation as a means to an end and that end was, as Zeno put it, to live consistently (δμολογουμενως ζπν), or as it was later explained, to live in conformity with nature. This conforming of the life to nature (δμολογουμενως τπ ϕυσει ζπν) was the Stoic idea of Virtue. ([Location 84](https://readwise.io/to_kindle?action=open&asin=B00RWJPS0E&location=84))

- In order to live in accord with nature, it is necessary to know what nature is; and to this end a threefold division of philosophy is made—into Physics, dealing with the universe and its laws, the problems of divine government and teleology; Logic, which trains the mind to discern true from false; and Ethics, which applies the knowledge thus gained and tested to practical life. ([Location 87](https://readwise.io/to_kindle?action=open&asin=B00RWJPS0E&location=87))

- Two points in the Stoic system deserve special mention. One is a careful distinction between things which are in our power and things which are not. Desire and dislike, opinion and affection, are within the power of the will; whereas health, wealth, honour, and other such are generally not so. The Stoic was called upon to control his desires and affections, and to guide his opinion; to bring his whole being under the sway of the will or leading principle, just as the universe is guided and governed by divine Providence.
